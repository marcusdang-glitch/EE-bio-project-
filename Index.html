Procedure outline (edit me)

  1. Grow green onions in water (control) and in each auxin solution.
  2. Collect root tip samples every few days.
  3. Treat samples with acid.
  4. Prepare slides and observe under the microscope.
  5. Count dividing cells and total cells in each group.

Research proposal

How auxin concentration affects mitosis in green onion

1 Question 2 Key terms 3 Why 4 Literature 5 Approach 6 References

01

Research question

Will green onions be affected by different amounts of auxin in terms of cell mitosis, and what is the range and degree of the impact?

02

The three key elements

Auxin
The most important variable in my research question. Auxin changes the growth of the plant and the process of mitosis.
Green onions
My experiment object, because green onions grow very quickly and we see them in daily life. I will take samples every few days and compare them with the control group.
Mitosis
The process of cell division. Most cells are in interphase, so I will compare the different groups and make a detailed graph of how much auxin affected the plants.

03

Why I chose this topic

I have heard that many sellers and farmers use auxins to grow more plants in a short period of time; in China there are many different methods of using plant growth chemicals. I have also seen farmers add auxin when a plant grows slowly, gets sick or stops growing, and this can make the fruit or vegetable taste worse. I have also learned that auxin has a connection to photosynthesis.

I am using green onion because it is common in daily life and grows fast, so the effect of auxin should be more visible. There are many kinds of auxin, and I am still deciding which one to buy.

04

Literature review

Low auxin concentrations speed up growth, while high concentrations can make the plant stop growing. In the low-concentration groups, MI, MCN and CAF all increased; in the high-concentration groups, the MI and MCN groups did not grow.

MIMitotic Index MCNMicronucleus rate CAFChromosomal Aberration Frequency

Different types of plants respond differently. My research will look not only at visible plant growth but also at how the cells divide.

Research approach

  • Equipment: microscope, beakers, scale
  • Safety: gloves, goggles
